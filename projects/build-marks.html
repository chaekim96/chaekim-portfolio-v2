Giving sixteen builds one family of logos.

A decision log. Four directions, three hybrids, six palettes, and the reasons behind each cut.

Role Designer and decision maker, with Claude Code as chief design officer · Scope Marks, palette, share cards, 15 repos · Timeline October 25, 2026

Start Scope House system Directions Hybrids Palettes White Electric Result Rules

Start from the brand I already have.

I asked for a logo for every build and work item. The Stamp personal brand was already the umbrella: ink #111110, off-white, seal red #C63D2F, Fraunces and Poppins, and the brush 김채운 for my name.

💡

The rule: the brush name never appears on a product. The products carry their own marks, and a small seal-red square says "a Chae Kim build."

Decide who gets a mark before drawing any.

Sixteen builds got marks: AI Visibility Checker, Tally, TypeRace, Palette Generator, Networking Tracker, Anatomy of a Website, VoicePrompter, Parallel Prospect Brief, SmartModel, Token-Fuel, Code Improver, Spotify Review Triage, Personal Wiki, Pac-Man DQN, Custom LLM, and Arcade.

  • Kept outside: Lucent has its own brand. MirrorMe is a printed product with pre-orders.
  • Left out: the stealth mobility project, archive pieces, and employers. Employers' brands aren't mine to redraw.
  • Third-party names: Spotify Review Triage, Parallel Prospect Brief, and Pac-Man DQN avoid those companies' logos and colors.

Version 1: one ink tile, one accent per build.

Every mark sits on the same 64-unit ink tile with a 14-unit corner, and each build gets one accent no other build uses. Three tiers set the wordmark face: products in Poppins 600, tools in JetBrains Mono, studies in Fraunces italic.

Each pictogram encodes what the build does. Tally is four strokes and a fifth slash. Token-Fuel is a fuel gauge at 67%. Spotify Review Triage is a funnel to one dot. Custom LLM is a training-loss curve. SmartModel is one prompt branching to four models.

I checked every mark at 16px and redrew two. The palette fan spilled to the edge of the tile, and the DQN agent read as a bracket.

Four directions, judged at 16px.

I asked for alternatives to the ink tile, using the same pictograms and accents so I could judge the treatment and not the drawings.

  • A · Ink tile: the live version. Reads as one family, but sixteen dark squares can feel heavy on a light portfolio.
  • B · Paper and ink: closest to Stamp, but quiet in a dark browser tab.
  • C · Color seal: the accent fills the tile and the glyph is drawn in ink. Boldest, and each build is recognisable by color alone.
  • D · Monogram: two letters. Easiest to extend, but it says nothing about what the build does and blurs at 16px.

Fold the monogram into the color seal, or not.

I liked C and asked whether D could be folded in. That gave three hybrids.

  • E · Seal and chop: the monogram sits in a small ink chop in the corner, like a painter's seal.
  • F · Monogram seal: the letters sit on the color.
  • G · Size switch: the pictogram for favicons, the monogram for large sizes.

I stuck with C.

📌

Lesson: letters never survive 16px, and 16px is where most people meet these marks. Every hybrid put letters somewhere that gets small.

Six palettes for the color seal.

Same drawings, six sets of colors. Each glyph switches between ink and white, whichever has more contrast with the tile.

  • Digital brights: the current palette, clean screen colors.
  • Obangsaek: the Korean five-direction colors in mineral-pigment tones. It shares a root with the brush signature.
  • Field instrument, Warm crema, Center court: from the directions in the personal brand case study.
  • Risograph: named riso inks, the loudest option.

I picked Digital brights. I still liked how Obangsaek worked white and brightness in, so I asked for that next.

Work white into Digital brights.

Five variations, each borrowing something from Obangsaek.

  • Deep brights, white glyph: deeper tiles with white drawings.
  • Brights with a white spark: the one detail that carries the idea turns white.
  • Obang balance: deep tiles with white glyphs, light tiles with ink, and a few white tiles.
  • White seal, color rim: white tiles with a colored rim.
  • Lit brights: a soft white light from the top of the tile.

I asked to combine White spark and Lit brights. I call it the lit spark. Any tile too pale for the white detail is deepened until white reaches 1.8:1 against it.

Try the lit spark in five palettes, then pick Electric.

I ran the lit spark across Digital brights, Electric, Jewel brights, Lagoon, and Sunset. Electric uses full-saturation colors, like system colors on a phone. I picked it.

Then I tried Electric 12% and 22% deeper. At 12%, every ink drawing stayed at 3.5:1 or better. At 22%, the ink drawing on violet dropped to 2.9:1, below the 3:1 bar for graphics. I looked at both and kept the original Electric.

💡

The rule: an ink drawing on a tile must keep at least 3:1 contrast. The 22% version failed it on violet, so the range stops short of there.

Roll it out to all 15 repos.

The Electric lit-spark color seals went to all 15 repos: favicons, home-screen icons, share cards, and GitHub social previews. Live sites get a 1200×630 share card. Repos without a site get a 1280×640 GitHub social preview. The files kept the same paths as version 1, so no page markup changed the second time.

Small rules came out of the process.

  • Judge marks at 16px in a tab bar, not at the size of the sheet.
  • One script generates every icon and card from one source. Switching direction is a regeneration, not a redesign.
  • Multicolor marks (Palette, Anatomy, Arcade) keep a dark tile and recolor their parts from the palette.
  • Glyphs switch between ink and white by contrast, and white details are deepened until they reach 1.8:1.

What happened next.

  • A new build gets a mark by adding a pictogram and an accent to the one script, then regenerating.
  • Every option behind these calls is on How I decide. The brand these marks sit under is in the personal brand case study.
