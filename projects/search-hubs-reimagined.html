Part 1 · The thought piece

Why Search Hubs should answer.

For most of the past year, I worked on a question brands didn't use to ask.

When someone asks ChatGPT about your category, does it mention you? That was the work at Lucent, an AI search visibility platform. Brands used to fight for the top blue link. Now they want to be the sentence an AI says out loud.

The same shift is happening inside TikTok. People type questions into TikTok search, not just keywords. And TikTok has something no search engine has: real people showing the product working on camera.

"TikTok,
just tell me."

People already search here.

Adobe's 2026 consumer survey found that 49% of U.S. consumers have used TikTok as a search engine, up from 41% in 2024. But only 4% of Gen Z said they prefer it to Google, down from 8%.1

People come to TikTok to see it, then go somewhere else to decide. The search habit is there. The answer isn't.

TikTok already sells the top of search.

At TikTok World in May 2026, TikTok announced Search Hubs: brand-owned pages at the top of search results.2 They're part of the Brand Discovery Bundle, with Branded Buzz, which produces creator videos at scale, and Keyword Amplifier, which adds clickable search suggestions to those videos. The pieces fit together: creators get people talking, people search, and the hub is waiting at the top.

Trade coverage describes several hub types at different stages: a Branded Search Hub that's generally available, a Category Search Hub in open beta, and a Sponsor Search Hub in alpha.3

But a hub is still a shelf.

Search "is Brightwell OK for sensitive skin?" and today's hub shows the brand's header, a few links, and a shelf of videos. The answer is in there, spread across clips. The searcher has to watch them to piece it together, or leave to ask somewhere else.

The bundle does the hard part, getting people to search for the brand. Then it hands them a page instead of an answer, right at the moment they're ready to decide.

The revamp: a hub that answers.

Add an answer module to the top of the hub. When the search is a question, the hub opens with a short answer built from creator videos. Every line carries a number, and every number opens the creator's video at the second that backs it up. The brand's own facts (price, ingredients, where to buy) fill in the details, labeled as coming from the brand.

Two rules make it trustworthy. If the creators disagree, the answer says so, even on the brand's own hub. And if there aren't enough good videos, there's no answer, just the shelf. A brand can choose not to turn the module on. It can't edit what it says.

Brands supply facts. Creators supply proof.

Brands set up the module with an Answer Brief in Ads Manager: approved facts, claims they can stand behind, and claims they can't make. TikTok never writes a brand claim that the brand didn't approve or a creator didn't show.

Creators opt in to being sources. A cited creator gets a "Source" badge, the views that come from the answer, and a share of the revenue when their video answered a hub search. They can see which questions their videos answered, which is a better content brief than any trend report.

How it makes money.

Search Hubs are already sold. The answer makes each type worth more:

  • Branded Search Hub: answers as a premium tier. Same hub, plus the answer module and its reporting. The easiest yes, because it upgrades something brands already buy.
  • Category Search Hub: a neutral answer, sponsored around it. For "best sunscreen for oily skin," the answer stays neutral across brands, and brands buy the sponsored slots beneath it.
  • Shop from the answer. When the answer names a product, checkout through TikTok Shop. Commission revenue, once people trust the answers.

I'd lead with the Branded premium tier. It's an upgrade, not a new product to explain, and it proves the answers work before TikTok sells anything next to them.

The pitch.

Most brands still think of TikTok as the top of the funnel: you go there to get noticed, and people decide somewhere else. Answers in Search Hubs change the story from "show up first" to "answer first." If I were positioning it, the statement would be:

For brands whose customers ask questions on TikTok before they buy, Search Hubs turn your brand page into the answer. Built from real creator videos, backed by facts you approve, and measured through to purchase. Unlike a static page or a search snippet, the answer comes from the creators people already trust.

And one message for each audience:

AdvertisersYour hub doesn't just show up first. It answers first.
Sales teamSell it as the upgrade to a hub they already run, with one new number: repeat searches down.
CreatorsYour honest review becomes the answer, and you get paid when it is.

Can TikTok actually do this?

Most of the pieces exist. Search Hubs and Keyword Amplifier already connect creator videos to search. TikTok captions videos automatically, so it knows what's said in each clip, and its Symphony AI tools already generate ad copy. It has labeled AI-made content with C2PA Content Credentials since 2024.4 The answer module is a new layer on top of those.

The hard part isn't the technology. It's accuracy and trust: knowing when not to answer, and keeping what's paid clearly apart from what isn't.

Questions I'd expect.

If I were presenting this at TikTok, these are the questions I'd expect in the room, and how I'd answer them.

Why would a brand want an answer that might criticize it?

Because the criticism is already on TikTok, one search away. A hub that admits "one tester saw redness" and then answers with the brand's facts is more believable than a shelf of ads. Brands that don't want that can leave the module off.

What happens when the answer is wrong?

Every line is tied to a source, so a wrong line can be traced and pulled. Health, money, and safety questions get no composed answer at launch, only source videos. Viewers and brands can flag a line, which sends it back for review.

Won't this take views away from creators?

That's the number I'd watch most closely. The answer is short on purpose, and every claim is a tap from the clip. If source taps don't hold up, the answer is too long.

On a Category hub, can a brand buy its way into the answer?

No. That's the one rule. Brands can pay for the slots next to the answer and for their facts to be right, never to change what creators said or which videos get cited.

Isn't this just Google's AI Overviews?

AI Overviews summarize web pages. This summarizes people showing the product on camera, and every claim opens the video. No one else has that.

What about AI-generated videos as sources?

AI-labeled videos aren't eligible as sources at launch. If the proof is the point, the proof has to be real.

How does this work with TikTok's U.S. setup?

TikTok's U.S. joint venture, formed in January 2026, retrains the recommendation system on U.S. data under Oracle's oversight.5 An answer module would need to be built and reviewed inside that setup, so the pilot might start in other markets first.

The rollout

Follow the hubs TikTok is already releasing.

Phase 1Branded hub pilot

Answers for a small group of brands already running Branded Search Hubs, in beauty, food, and travel.

"Is Brightwell OK for sensitive skin?"
Phase 2Category hubs

Neutral answers to category questions, with sponsored slots beneath them, as Category hubs leave beta.

"Best sunscreen for oily skin"
Phase 3Sponsor hubs

Test answers on Sponsor hubs once the format has proven it keeps paid and unpaid apart.

The first answer next to a sponsor.
Phase 4Self-serve

Answer Briefs in Ads Manager and Smart+, with an API for agencies running many brands.
